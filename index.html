When help has to arrive, nothing may fail quietly.

We build the layer that carries an emergency from the person in danger to the responder who can reach them, and refuses to fail in silence.

An ordinary safety app works only under ideal conditions: a lit screen, a live network, a calm hand. The moments that matter most are the opposite of that.

SecureLink is engineered for those moments. It raises an alarm from a pocket with the screen off, reaches the responsible agency even when the internet is gone, and proves, at every step, whether help has actually been engaged. A person is never told help is coming until a responder has truly accepted the case.

How the platform earns its trust.

Resilience

Locked screens, terminated processes, aggressive battery management, and no signal at all. The failure modes that break ordinary safety products are the conditions we design against first.

Orchestration

An emergency is not one undifferentiated cry for help. Each incident is classified and routed to the agency that owns it, scoped to where it happened, and escalated the moment no one answers.

Accountability

Every incident moves through an auditable chain, from triggered to routed to delivered to acknowledged to resolved. Reaching a server is never confused with reaching a responder.

Sovereignty

Infrastructure for African emergencies, built and governed in Africa, with a privacy model institutions can trust and no dependence on systems that can be switched off from elsewhere.

The platform

SecureLink is an emergency orchestration platform.

Not a panic button, but a connected system: a client that survives on the phone, a backend that routes and escalates, an offline path over GSM, and an operator platform bounded by audit. Every alert becomes a stateful incident that moves through a defined chain until someone takes responsibility for it.

Triggered› Received› Triaged› Routed› Delivered› Acknowledged› Responding› On scene› Resolved

If acknowledgement does not come in time, the incident escalates automatically, paging a widening circle of responders until the case is owned. Routing tells you who should come; this is what makes sure someone does.

What the platform is engineered to do.

Raise the alarm without the screen

Hardware-key and motion triggers that fire from a pocket, a persistent guard that resists being killed by the operating system, and recovery after reboot, validated against the Android devices most common across the continent.

An alert when the network is gone

When data fails, a structured alert travels over GSM to a shared alert network and to the user's guardians, with location captured on the device, so the coordinates are real even with no connection.

Follow a moving emergency

Tracking continues after the first alert, so a responder navigates to where a person is now, not a pin frozen where the incident began, with short audio captured as it unfolds.

Privacy institutions can sign

Operators see incident metadata, never victim content. Location, audio, and movement trails require a written reason, a time limit, and an entry in a permanent audit log, defensible to agencies and regulators alike.

Aligned with a national priority.

Nigeria is consolidating emergency coordination around a single national number, 112, with a multi-agency implementation structure and Emergency Communications Centres established across the country.

SecureLink does not set out to replace that architecture. Its purpose is to complement it, to be a trusted incident source and orchestration layer that strengthens the national response ecosystem and helps organisations run their own emergency workflows within it. The opportunity is to work with the emergency ecosystem, not around it.
